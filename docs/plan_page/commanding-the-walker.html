gait_planner · full-pose planner

A planner that sketches motion, a muscle policy that carries it out

A diffusion planner draws the next 1.2 s of whole-body motion. The 90-muscle policy follows only its pelvis, foot and trunk targets.

Why

Separate where to go from how to move

Learn motion from data

Trained on 13,379 human clips, the planner knows what walking, turning, stairs and steps look like.

Let the muscles decide the joints

Generated poses can ask for moves our muscle model can't make. The policy gets targets, not poses.

Command it, don't retrain it

A new speed, direction or terrain changes the planner's input, not the muscle policy.

How · the planner

One window in, 1.2 seconds out

Everything is placed relative to the anchor: the pelvis at the last given frame, turned to its 1 s average heading.

How · what keeps it physical

Four design choices

Borrowed from large motion models (Kimodo, MotionBricks) and sized for our data.

1 · Absolute heights

One zero per plan: the ground under the anchor

Pelvis, foot points and the terrain patch are all measured from the ground under the anchor's pelvis. Heights measured per point would jump at every step edge.

2 · Foot points + contact

Feet that agree with the joints and stay planted

The planner also outputs heel and ball points. Training ties them to the feet its own joint angles make, and holds still any point the data has on the ground.

3 · Terrain penetration

No foot inside the ground

Every foot sphere, placed by the joint angles, may sit at most 1 cm under the terrain patch. Using the lowest nearby cell keeps box edges sharp.

4 · Smoothed heading

A steady frame to plan in

Each plan is laid out along the pelvis heading averaged over the last second, so replans don't twist with every step.

How · inside the planner

A diffusion transformer

Terrain and frames become one row of tokens. The diffusion step and the goal steer every layer.

How · training

One training step: noise the future, recover it

The data's own future is the answer key. Foot geometry is scored too.

Signal share ā

Score feet only when the guess is sharp

At heavy noise the clean guess is a blur, so one exact foot height is not a fair target.

Huber

Squared up to 2 cm, straight beyond

A few far-off points can't take over the gradient.

Optimiser

120,000 steps, best held-out kept

  • AdamW, learning rate 2e-4, cosine decay to 0
  • batch 512, gradient clip 1, bfloat16
  • EMA 0.995: a running average of the weights, used for sampling
  • kept: lowest held-out loss (held-out trials and terrain, incl. 6 stair clips)
How · sampling

Draw a plan, pin the feet, keep a little, plan again

Foot hold

A planted foot stays planted across replans

Each foot point on the ground at the end of the history stays put until the plan's own contact flag lifts it.

How · commanding it

A command is a goal point

Speed sets how far ahead the pelvis should be in 1.2 s. The direction is fixed in the world, so the walker can't wander off it.

1.44 m aheadturn once, then straight
How · with the muscle policy

Two loops at two speeds

Each plan starts from the body's measured state, so a drift or stumble is corrected at the next replan. For now the planner is rolled out alone: its own kept frames become the next history.

gait_planner · full-pose planner

A planner that sketches motion, a muscle policy that carries it out

A diffusion planner draws the next 1.2 s of whole-body motion. The 90-muscle policy follows only the pelvis, foot and trunk targets from it.

Plain planner, 17 h of motion: training, done ~7:50 pm This design (v2): starts after it, done ~10:15 pm
Why

Separate where to go from how to move

Learn motion from data

A diffusion model trained on human motion knows what walking, turning and stepping onto terrain look like.

Let the muscles decide the joints

Generated poses can contain transitions our muscle model can't make. The policy gets targets, not poses, and finds joint motion it can actually produce.

Command it, don't retrain it

A new speed, direction or terrain changes the planner's input, not the muscle policy.

How · the planner

One window in, 1.2 seconds out

Positions are relative to the pelvis at "now" and its smoothed heading. Heights are measured from the ground directly underneath.

How · what keeps it physical

Four design choices

Borrowed from large motion models (Kimodo, MotionBricks) and sized for our data.

1 · Absolute heights

Heights from the ground, not from the body

The pelvis and every foot point carry their height above the terrain directly below. The floor can't drift away between replans.

2 · Foot points + contact losses

Feet that agree with the joints and stay planted

The planner also outputs heel and ball positions. Training checks them against the feet computed from its own joint angles, and holds still any point the data has on the ground.

3 · Goal guidance

Follow the command more firmly

Each step predicts with and without the goal and pushes twice as far toward the goal. Rare fast commands stop drifting back to the average speed.

4 · Smoothed heading

A steady frame to plan in

Each plan is laid out along the pelvis heading averaged over the last second, not its momentary yaw, so replans don't twist with every step.

How · commanding it

A command is a goal point

Speed sets how far ahead the pelvis should be in 1.2 s. Direction sets where, and it doesn't have to be where the body faces.

How · forward simulation

Two loops at two speeds

The planner always starts from the body's measured state, so a drift or stumble is corrected at the next replan.

How · over time

Plan far, use a little, plan again

Each plan reaches 1.2 s ahead; the policy follows only its first 0.2 s. The far end keeps the near end sensible.

Footstep planner ladder

Seven designs for how the muscle model decides where its feet go. Each one changes a single thing from the one before, so every rung answers one question. The first real rung is Watch Your Step's blind guesser, so the page starts with why that works at all.

Why a blind guesser works in Watch Your Step

The guesser never decides where a foot lands. It only decides where the policy looks. The terrain still reaches it, through a loop that runs through the body.

It is redone every 20 ms

The guess is recomputed 20 to 30 times per step from joint angles, joint speeds, base velocity and the command. When a patch shows a stair edge and the policy shortens the swing, the body changes, and the next guess moves with it.

It learns from real landings

Its labels are where the policy's feet actually landed, terrain adjustments included. Stair posture (more knee bend, upward base speed) teaches it stair-sized steps without seeing a stair.

Its misses fit inside the patch

The paper's stones are 30 to 50 cm and its gaps 10 to 20 cm, so most corrections fit inside one patch.

Where it breaks

A landing far from the guess

Wide gaps, high step-ups, the first step onto a big feature. The guess assumes a normal stride, so the patch can show the pit instead of the far edge.

Where it breaks

Planning past two steps

People change step length two or three steps before stairs or an obstacle. Two patches show two footholds, so the policy reacts. It can succeed while correcting later than a person would.

What it never tested

Human-like placement

The paper's claims are faster training and better transfer on a G1 robot with joint position control. Whether the feet land where a person's would is our question, not theirs.

The reward in B1 (L0 and L1)

One number per control step, 40 times a second: half the paper's task terms, half the style judge's score, minus what the muscles cost. L4 adds a following term and L5, L6 the plan's own terms; those are still to design.

r =½ × task+½ × style−effort

The most a walking step can earn, to scale. A fall costs 5, once; the effort and the penalties below come off.

Style the judge, retrained every iteration

Stylemax(0, 1 − ¼ (D − 1)²), D the judge's score+0.5

The judge sees two poses 25 ms apart: pelvis height over the ground, the pelvis's up axis, the 17 joint angles and both heels relative to the pelvis, plus how far the pelvis moved and turned between them.

It learns to score 75,334 pairs from 198 AB01 clips (walking, start-stop, standing; mirrored half the time) toward +1 and the policy's latest pairs toward −1, in 80 updates per iteration.

Effort as our other muscle studies weigh it

Metabolic powerW per kg of body mass−0.02
Cubed activationΣ a³ over the 90 muscles−0.02
Compartment balanceΣ variance / mean² within a split muscle (gluteus maximus 1–3, …)−0.02

Without the balance term, the near-linear metabolic cost lets one compartment do its group's work.

How we'd judge them

Human-likeness only means something on terrain we have people walking on, and against subjects held out from training.

Step length before the first stair sketch

No numbers on purpose: the human curve will come from Camargo's level-to-stair trials. This is the measure that should split the reactive designs from the planned ones.

Foot placement

Step length and width against speed; where the foot lands on a tread and how far it clears the nosing; take-off distance from a platform edge.

Anticipation

Step length over the 3 steps before a terrain change, against Camargo's transition trials (the sketch).

Biomechanics

Joint angles, ground forces, joint moments and EMG against Camargo, terrain by terrain.

Task

Falls, terrain level reached, and how fast each design learns.

Guesser misses

Foothold error by terrain, and how often the foot lands outside its patch. This is where L1 should fail first.

Caveat

Copied isn't predicted

From L3 up, foot placement comes from human data, so it can't be claimed as a prediction. The claims are what wasn't copied: forces, moments, EMG, and behaviour on terrain past the clips.

Data

No SEED in footstep models

Guessers and planners train on AB01, Camargo and the captured PARC clips only. A model that predicts human footsteps is too close to a motion generator for SEED's licence.

Coverage

Past the clips

Stepping stones, boxes and pits have no human footstep data, so L3 to L6 extrapolate there. That's where L6's loop has to earn its place.

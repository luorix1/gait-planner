The whole flow

One loop to ground the planner, one to run it

ProblemHuman clips show what looks human, but not what the 90-muscle body can carry out.

What counts as success
Plausible

Looks human, breaks no physics

  • Matches held-out human motion on the same terrain: joint angles, step length, contact timing.
  • No sinking, sliding, floating or teleports; accelerations the feet can produce.
Executable

The muscle body carries it out

  • The teacher tracks the plans in simulation.
  • The student follows the planner's slice live without falling, at human-like effort.
What learns

Four networks; everything else is fixed code

The planner and the style prior learn from human clips. The teacher and the student learn to drive the muscles. Best-of-16, clean-up, physics + muscle check, keep filter and foot hold are fixed rules.

Why these designs
Planner

Why diffusion

A step can come now or a bit later, long or short. A network trained to predict one answer averages those into a shuffle; diffusion picks one whole, consistent option.

Planner

Why a transformer

Terrain and frames are tokens, so each future frame can look at the ground where that foot will land.

Teacher → student

Why two controllers

Tracking every joint of a plan is easy to learn. A deployable controller only gets where and when to step, so it learns by copying the teacher.

Style prior

Why learned style

"Looks human" is hard to write as rules; a model of human clips scores it directly. It measures familiarity, though (below).

Style prior

a judge of how familiar a short piece of motion looks
How well it judges motion
99.8 %a held-out real window scores above a corrupted copy of itself (faster, skating, crouched)
50–100 %of each held-out person's real windows fall below a cut-off that only 5 % of corrupted copies pass
100 %of real 10° ramp walking rejected by a prior that never saw 10° ramps (11-16 test)
Useas a reward and to compare checkpoints: yes. As a yes/no gate on new terrain: no

Decided 11-22: a reward and a drift monitor on familiar ground, not a veto. On new terrain it would reject real motion, so the P3 keep filter judges muscle use instead.

P0~1 day

A scoreboard before any new run

Held-out loss is noisy and ignores terrain. Every checkpoint and gate is judged by rollouts instead.

ProblemLoss cannot tell a checkpoint that plants feet on a tread from one that sinks them into it.

Why these choices
1Fixed test casesHeld-out windows and fixed courses: every checkpoint faces the same test.
2Rollouts, not lossEvery 5k iterations, fixed seeds. A rollout shows where the feet meet the ground; held-out loss does not.
3One benchmark for every phasePlanner, library, loop rounds and closed loop on one scale.

P23–4 days

A library of plans, and a teacher that tracks them

PARC's recipe with our parts: generate, keep the best, clean up, check, then try it on the muscle body.

ProblemP3 needs plans on terrain the clips never covered, and a muscle-body policy that can carry them out. Today's teacher has only been tested on AB01.

Why these choices
1Best of 16Samples vary; score each on penetration, contact, seam, jerk and goal, and keep the cleanest.
2Clean-upFix small flaws (jerk, sliding, feet off the surface) while staying close to the plan.
3Physics + muscle checkDrop plans no body could do: an acceleration the feet cannot push, joints past their limits, or joint torques these muscles cannot make (inverse dynamics with ground forces estimated from the contacts, then static optimisation). Seconds per plan, no policy involved. Necessary, not sufficient: it ignores activation dynamics and delays.
4Teacher from the AB01 teacherStarts from a policy that already tracks (4.9° · 2.2 % falls) and practises most where it fails.

Risk: one teacher tracking the whole SEED library has never worked for us. Earlier policies fell on every clip, but they used an older recipe; the current teacher recipe has only been tested on AB01. Fallback: SEED's walking, stairs and box subsets only.

Why these choices
1Wider terrain each roundPushes past the clips' terrain as far as the body succeeds. Plans first pass P2's physics + muscle check, so impossible ones never reach the simulator.
2Teacher fine-tuned each roundOn the round's plans, replaying its failures more, as PARC does with its tracker. A plan that still fails is then more likely impossible than the teacher too weak.
3Keep filter: muscle use, not styleNo fall; tracking error ≤ 8° · ≤ 5 cm; little time at maximum activation; co-contraction and energy per metre in human ranges; EMG on/off timing of key muscles near the references. On new terrain these are bounds, not matches: no human EMG exists for it.
4Real ≥ 2/3, kept ≤ 1/3 of each batchReal data stays in every batch as the human anchor.

The deployable policy follows the planner live

The planner replans from the simulated body, so an infeasible stretch is corrected 0.2 s later instead of piling up.

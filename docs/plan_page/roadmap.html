Plausible

Looks human, breaks no physics

  • Matches held-out human motion on the same terrain: joint angles, step length, contact timing.
  • No sinking, sliding, floating or teleports; accelerations the feet can produce.
Executable

The muscle body carries it out

  • The teacher tracks the plans in simulation.
  • The student follows the planner's slice live without falling, at human-like effort.
What learns

Four networks; everything else is fixed code

The planner and the style prior learn from human clips. The teacher and the student learn to drive the muscles. Best-of-16, clean-up, physics check, keep filter and foot hold are hand-written rules.

Planner

a diffusion transformer that turns noise into the next 1.2 s

Teacher, student and style prior

two muscle controllers of the same shape, and a judge of human-likeness
Planner

Why diffusion

A step can come now or a bit later, long or short. A network trained to predict one answer averages those into a shuffle; diffusion picks one whole, consistent option.

Planner

Why a transformer

Terrain and frames are tokens, so each future frame can look at the ground where that foot will land.

Teacher → student

Why two controllers

Tracking every joint of a plan is easy to learn. A deployable controller only gets where and when to step, so it learns by copying the teacher.

Style prior

Why learned style

"Looks human" is hard to write as rules; a model of human clips scores it directly.

P0~1 day

A scoreboard before any new run

Held-out loss is noisy and ignores terrain. Every checkpoint and gate is judged by rollouts instead.

ProblemLoss cannot tell a checkpoint that plants feet on a tread from one that sinks them into it.

1Fixed test casesHeld-out windows and fixed courses: every checkpoint faces the same test.
2Rollouts, not lossEvery 5k iterations, fixed seeds. A rollout shows where the feet meet the ground; held-out loss does not.
3One benchmark for every phasePlanner, library, loop rounds and closed loop on one scale.
P12–3 days · 2–3 runs of ~2 h

The planner: lean on terrain, not on memory

Given the past 0.4 s and an exact goal, the planner memorised its two minutes of stairs instead of learning to read them. So we give it weaker clues, make the ground a direct target, and keep the terrain in charge when sampling. The ideas come from PARC, CAMDM, SCENIC and PRIMAL.

ProblemWith that much information, the planner recognises which training clip it is in and replays what came next instead of reading the terrain.

Fix 1 · training windows

make history and goal weaker clues
1Goal at a random 0.4–1.2 s horizon, + 5 cm noiseThe horizon is an input, so the goal means head about here instead of a clip's exact pelvis position.
2Noise = smooth whole-body offset, re-run through FKA noisy history is still a body a policy could produce. Before, per-channel noise made joints and foot positions disagree.
3Hide the history in 15 % of windowsThe model must learn what terrain + goal alone call for. This also trains the history-free prediction that sampling mixes in (Fix 3).

Fix 2 · terrain

make the foot's relation to the ground a direct target
45 cm cells near the feetA 22 cm tread was only 2 cells at 10 cm.
5Contact points pulled onto the surfaceBefore, they only matched the data's height.
6Penetration on the exact heightfieldThe lowest of 4 coarse cells missed about 20 % of stair stance spheres.
7Random terrain in 10 % of batchesPenetration loss only: teaches never put feet in the ground beyond the few real terrains.

Fix 3 · sampling

keep the terrain in charge at run time
810 denoising stepsWas 50: 3.4 s instead of 17.2 s for 2,048 plans.
9With and without history, mixed 65/35Each step runs the network twice and leans 65 % toward the with-history prediction. Since s < 1 it leans away from the history; ordinary guidance (s > 1) would amplify it. 0.65 is PARC's value, untested here; it only affects sampling, so a trained checkpoint can be swept.
10Joints are the truthAfter step 10, FK rebuilds the segment poses, so a plan is always one consistent body.
11Foot hold, then execute 0.2 sA few optimiser steps on the leg joints keep planted feet where they are until the plan's contact flag lifts them; the next history is rebuilt from the joints.
!CostWeaker history could make replan seams rougher. The foot hold compensates, and the scoreboard's seam metric measures it.
17.2 snow: 2,048 plans at 50 steps (4090, measured)
3.4 ssame, 10 steps (measured)
≤ 0.5 sneeded: 4,096 plans, for training with the planner in the loop (P4)

P23–4 days

A library of plans, and a teacher that tracks them

PARC's recipe with our parts: generate, keep the best, clean up, check, then try it on the muscle body.

ProblemP3 needs plans on terrain the clips never covered, and a muscle-body policy that can carry them out. Today's teacher has only been tested on AB01.

1Best of 16Samples vary; score each on penetration, contact, seam, jerk and goal, and keep the cleanest.
2Clean-upFix small flaws (jerk, sliding, feet off the surface) while staying close to the plan.
3Physics checkDrop plans no body could do: an acceleration the feet cannot push, joints past their limits.
4Teacher from the AB01 teacherStarts from a policy that already tracks (4.9° · 2.2 % falls) and practises most where it fails.

Risk: one teacher tracking the whole SEED library has never worked for us. Earlier policies fell on every clip, but they used an older recipe; the current teacher recipe has only been tested on AB01. Fallback: SEED's walking, stairs and box subsets only.

Ideas for our planner

1

Physics-correct and augment rare terrain in our own simulator

  • Roll a tracker (the muscle policy, or a cheaper torque tracker) over planner outputs on stairs and boxes; keep the runs that finish; re-record kinematics and contacts; retrain.
  • PARC ablation: penetration 547 → 180, high-jerk frames 18.7 → 2.7 %. High jerk is what a teleport looks like.
  • Zhang–Hutter: 5 min → 1 h by rescaling stairs and boxes, re-optimising, then physics refinement.
2

More real stair and ramp data before synthetic

  • AddBiomechanics re-solves Camargo on a model with spine DoFs, dynamics-consistent.
  • Gait120, Darmstadt stairs, Vielemeyer ramps (table above).
  • TERRA rebuilds stair heights from where the feet rest, then retargets with penetration constraints. This targets our Camargo heel and sinking problems directly.
3

Terrain-relative contact loss, random-terrain steps

  • PARC: contact feet's signed distance to the real heightmap pushed to 0, stricter than a height loss.
  • 10 % of steps on random terrain with penetration loss only.
  • Best of N by penetration + contact at sampling; 10 DDIM steps make it affordable (CLoSD: 10 is enough; Zhang–Hutter: 2).
4

Close the loop

  • Build the history from the simulated state, not the previous plan (CLoSD: 0.19 → 0.88).
  • Noise on history and heightmap in training (Zhang–Hutter).
  • PARC's blend with / without previous frames (s = 0.65); their failure was ignoring terrain by leaning on history.
  • Fine-tune the policy against the frozen planner (CLoSD, Zhang–Hutter).
5

Align the planner with a critic

  • Train v(plan slice, heightmap) on muscle-policy rollouts.
  • Use it as a fine-tuning loss (RobotMDM) or as sampling guidance (Trace & Pace).
  • With effort or metabolic cost in the critic's reward, plans drift toward ones the body runs cheaply.
6

GRF / COP output channels

  • GaitDynamics' representation; train only where force was measured (AddBiomechanics 57.6 h), mask the loss elsewhere.
  • Ties contact flags to force; gives the policy reward a GRF target.
  • Centroidal loss m(a_COM − g) = ΣGRF is the agent's own proposal; no paper uses it.
7

Box jumps

  • Planner + tracker is structurally weak at jumps (Diffuse-CLoC 0–22 % vs 71 %; PARC without correction changes direction mid-jump).
  • Cheap first: physics-corrected jump clips (idea 1). Joint state–action diffusion is stronger but costly with muscle actions.
8

The slice the policy sees

  • Published slices range from 2-D waypoints (PACER) to targets at +0.2–0.8 s (TERRA) to the full reference (Zhang–Hutter). Ours sits in between.
  • Give the policy its own heightmap: Zhang–Hutter's tracker sometimes overrode the reference to clear obstacles.
9

Evaluation protocol

  • Hold out whole studies as well as subjects (GaitDynamics: 7 unseen studies, 92 people).
  • Test terrain beyond the training range (Zhang–Hutter: 80 cm boxes).
  • PARC metrics: penetration, contact, high-jerk frames (threshold = dataset maximum, the teleport detector); plus GRF error in %BW.

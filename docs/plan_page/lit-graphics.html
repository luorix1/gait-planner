Differences worth trying

Ordered by how directly each targets our open problems: memorised stairs and boxes, the box snap, feet in treads.

1

Make new motion–terrain pairs, don't oversample

  • Every terrain paper starts small and augments before training.
  • PARC: 50 spatial variants per clip; heightmap max-pool and boxes away from the body; 10 % of steps on random terrain, penetration loss only.
  • 2604.17335: 5 min → ~1 h by rescaling boxes (35–75 cm) and steps (15–20 cm), adding small boxes, then optimising out penetration.
  • SCENIC: fits each clip to many patches, keeps the best 3, refines footholds, mirrors.
  • For us: vary riser and tread on our staircase, re-solve legs by IK, add random-terrain steps.
2

Lean less on history

  • PARC: terrain-ignoring motion on small data came from overfitting the previous frames. Fix: 15 % history masking + blended sampling (s = 0.65).
  • CAMDM: drops past motion 15 %, samples at γ = 0.7.
  • Ours: only the goal is dropped and w = 1, so guidance does nothing.
  • Most use 1–2 history frames (PRIMAL, PARC, DART, 2604.17335) or 0.33 s (CAMDM, SCENIC); ours is 0.4 s.
3

Train on the seam

  • DART: rollout training feeds the model its own history, like our replan.
  • UniPhys: per-frame noise, history kept slightly noisy at sampling; episode length 2320 vs 148 (with physics in the loop).
  • Cheap: PRIMAL's inertialization blend + ground snap at seams.
  • Counterpoint: PRIMAL found 0.01 history noise useless.
4

Heightmap guidance in the last denoising steps

  • SCENIC: at the final step, contact feet pulled onto the surface, everything else pushed out.
  • PhysDiff: corrections inside the last 4 steps beat correcting afterwards; post-hoc projection did not help.
  • Our heightmap allows an analytic lookup.
  • Also add a terrain-height target for contact feet to the foot hold, as PARC's optimisation does (penetration / contact weights 1000, 3000 Adam steps).
5

Sample a batch, keep the best

  • PARC: top 2 of 128 by target, contact and penetration.
  • A-MDM: 100 candidates. TRACE filters by guidance loss.
  • Nearly free on GPU. Add a seam-jump term to catch the snap.
6

Pick checkpoints by rollouts, not held-out loss

  • PARC: 3,200 motions on 100 test terrains; waypoint error, penetration, contact, share of high-jerk frames.
  • MDM keeps the lowest FID; CLoSD kept 200K of 600K; UniPhys notes the loss barely moves.
  • For us: rollouts on held-out stairs and boxes, scored on penetration, slip, goal error, jerk, seam jump.
7

Regularise, and loosen the goal

  • PARC defaults: 4 × 256 (far smaller than ours), dropout 0.1, history attention dropout 0.15, heightmap / target dropout 0.025, weight decay 0.01, history noise 0.01, goal noise 0.05.
  • PARC's goal is a direction from a random 0.4–1.5 s ahead. Our exact pelvis target at 1.2 s is nearly a unique key to a memorised clip.
  • Diffuse-CLoC: attention dropout 0.3.
8

Close the loop with physics, later

  • PARC: tracker success 27 → 68 % over its iterations.
  • CLoSD, 2604.17335: planner conditioned on simulated history; 2604.17335 fine-tunes the tracker against the frozen planner.
  • ReinDiffuse (2410.07296): PPO on skate / float / penetration rewards; penetration 0.048 → 0.000, FID 0.544 → 0.385.
9

Fewer diffusion steps, trained at that count

  • 8–21 steps in CAMDM, DART, PARC, Diffuse-CLoC.
  • Gain is speed for the RL loop; no evidence it helps quality.
10

Architecture: low priority

  • ACMDM (2505.19377) uses QK-norm, SwiGLU, adaLN too; adaLN beats concatenation. Our choices are mainstream.
  • With adaLN, x0 vs v prediction differ little (FID 0.133 vs 0.121).
  • GMD root emphasis (×10) halved foot skating under trajectory conditioning (0.284 → 0.128); only if goal adherence is weak.
Representation

Segment positions and orientations

How papers carry body segments, and which output they trust.

Positions primary

Fit rotations after

  • HumanML3D family: MDM, GMD, OmniControl, StableMoFusion, PhysDiff, CLoSD.
  • 263-d frame: root velocity + height, root-relative joint positions, velocities, 6D rotations, 4 contacts.
  • Meshes from SMPL fitting to joints. TRUMANS: positions only, SMPL-X fitted after.
Rotations primary + loss

Positions kept consistent by FK

  • PARC: all body positions + rotations + contacts, FK loss; code trusts rotations.
  • DART: 22 joint positions + 6D rotations.
  • 2604.17335: 23 link positions + joint angles.
  • SCENIC, CAMDM: no positions, FK loss only.
  • Ours sits here, with foot points only.
Rotations primary + recompute

Rebuild positions each step

  • PRIMAL rebuilds joint positions from rotations at every autoregressive step, because predicted joints drift from the body.
Physics resolves it

The simulator decides

  • Diffuse-CLoC (positions, velocities + PD actions), UniPhys, CLoSD.

No ablation shows redundant segment positions help foot contact or penetration.

  • DART claims joint positions reduce skating but does not test it.
  • MARDM (2411.16575): redundancy helps VQ models (FID 0.335 vs 0.418); removing it was crucial for their diffusion model (2.196 → 0.116), confounded by a new autoencoder.
  • ACMDM: redundancy helped x0 prediction slightly (0.969 → 0.771); plain absolute positions with v prediction did best (0.208); no contact metrics.
  • MDM: removing the foot loss scored slightly better but looked shakier.
  • Cheap step for us: PRIMAL-style re-projection, i.e. overwrite foot points with FK(angles) before feeding the history back.

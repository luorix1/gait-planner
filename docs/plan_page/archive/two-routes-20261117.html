Sparse-target planner

A muscle policy drives a musculoskeletal body over terrain, coached by a style prior learned from human motion. The pipeline comes in two versions. In A, a planner tells the policy where and when each foot lands. In B, the policy gets only a direction and speed and picks its own footholds, seeing the ground where it is about to step (after Heyrman et al. 2026). B is next; A is parked as the comparison.

At a glance

M

Muscle policy · both

Drives 90 muscles at 40 Hz in our GPU simulator. Joint angles are left free; nothing tells it the full pose.

A

Planner · A

Given the ground ahead and a goal, it proposes footholds, foot timing, a centre-of-mass band and the pelvis orientation, learned from human plans as PARC's generator is. The animation shows this version.

B

Foothold guesser · B

Predicts the next two footholds from the body's motion alone, so the policy sees the ground where it is about to step. Starts from human clips, then is refit to the policy's own steps.

✓

Coach and checks · both

A frozen style prior scores how human the motion looks and pays part of the reward. Checks against human forces, loads and contact decide which episodes become data in A, and only measure in B.

What a plan looks like (A)

Feet get a place and a time. The body gets a band, and the pelvis an orientation, which fixes where the hips are. The joints are free. Overlapping foot intervals mean walking, and a gap between them means flight, so the plan sets the gait without dictating the swing.

The policy sees its own body, the next second of the plan and the ground only around where the plan puts each foot down next: 7 × 7 heights 5 cm apart per foot (after Heyrman et al. 2026, whose policy learnt faster that way than from a whole map). A coarser strip of the ground ahead goes to the value network only. It is rewarded for contacts (0.15), footholds within 10 cm (0.15), staying in the band (0.10), keeping the pelvis within 6° of its planned orientation (0.10) and the style score (0.50), minus muscle effort.

Version A in detail: learn from real motion, then the loop

First every part learns from real motion. Then the loop grows the data: new ground, new plans, and only the episodes that pass the gatekeeper are kept.

Phase 1 · parked

Learn from real motion

The muscle policy follows plans copied from real clips, so a failure is the policy's, not the planner's. A full-pose tracker on the same clips, for the same hours and side by side, is the baseline. On v2 (8 h each) the baseline fell in 58 % of episodes and the planner policy in 80 %. On v4 (stopped after 5 h) they fell in 59 % and 64 %, after about 1.5 s on every kind of motion.

Phase 3 · each round · parked

The loop

New ground gets a little harder each round. Only episodes that pass the gatekeeper join the planner's data, next to the real clips; the planner is retrained from scratch on all of it and the policy keeps training. Built: the course generator, the planner, episodes on generated ground. Being rebuilt: the acceptance filter, with its own exam on real clips. Not built: the code that runs the rounds.

Open: the style score's place in the gate. It turned out to measure familiarity: a prior that never saw 10° ramps rejected every real 10° walk, and real PARC motion scored about as badly as crouched walking. So it coaches well (it prefers real motion over broken copies 99 % of the time) but cannot yet accept new ground. Prior variants are being compared on how well they grade real and broken motion (balanced sampling, the original's normaliser, priors told the situation, more people from Bones SEED). Decided 16 Nov: the prior is frozen, trained once on AB01 + PARC + SEED and never on kept episodes; whether it can gate new ground stays open, and you will review whether kept plans start to look non-human. In version B it is only a coach.

The six checks

In A they gate which episodes are kept; in B they only measure. An episode that falls is dropped first. Then it must pass every check; a check with no limit for that ground counts as not passed yet. Each limit comes from the same measure on real clips, compared like with like (per stance, per leg).

Being rebuilt (16 Nov), after a review: the old contact check measured walking progress rather than slip (every real held-out clip failed it), the force limits compared an episode's worst stance with single real stances, and ground without limits passed unchecked. The filter now gets its own exam, like the style prior's: real held-out clips must pass each check about 95 % of the time, and deliberately broken copies must fail.

Camargo 2021 (22 subjects; inverse dynamics redone on our body, matching their own at r 0.88–0.997) is the human reference for forces, moments and EMG. It is all walking, so running takes its limits from AB01's 2.5 minutes of treadmill running on force plates (inverse dynamics on the same body; the plates' 14 ms lag behind the cameras corrected). AB01 ran at 2.0–2.7 m/s while the data's running reaches about 4.5 m/s (Bones SEED's sprints), so the running limits rise with the target speed, following AB01's own trend plus a margin that widens the further they extrapolate. Jumps, drops and platforms have no force reference yet, so those checks stay pending there.

What it starts from

Every clip is retargeted onto one body. The training data (v4) is AB01, our pilot subject on treadmill and overground, PARC's 31 original platform clips, and Bones SEED (16.3 h from 426 people). Each dataset's share of training is set by the square root of its size, so SEED does not drown the others. Camargo 2021 stays out of training on purpose, as the human reference for forces and EMG.

What we can claim

The style prior makes the kinematics human by construction, so claims rest on forces, muscles and responses to change.

New ground

Stair heights and ramp grades outside the training set.

Human kinetics and EMG

Forces, joint moments and muscle on/off timing against Camargo's 22 subjects (EMG ceiling r 0.87–0.92).

Changed dynamics

Added load, ankle weakness and exo assistance, against published human responses.

Fair baseline

Full-pose tracking on the same clips and body, with the same training time.

Every step has been run end to end with tiny settings, and 18 tests pass. In the code, --task planner trains the muscle policy on plans copied from clips; the planner network trains on its own (step 6) and drives the policy once the loop runs.

Settings
  • Control at 40 Hz with 75 physics steps each (3 kHz), implicit contact, human motor delays.
  • Style prior: 13-frame windows (0.325 s), scored every step; half of each batch mirrored left to right; 200k iterations.
  • Muscle effort is metabolic, at Rajagopal's own 0.6 MPa specific tension (the default 0.25 MPa made muscles 2.4× too heavy).
  • A fall: pelvis more than 0.3 m from the clip's, or tilted over 60°, for 3 steps. On generated ground there is no clip: the centre of mass more than 0.5 m from the plan's, or the tilt.
  • Planner: a diffusion model over plan frames at 20 Hz (0.4 s given, 1.2 s generated), reading the ground on a 0.1 m grid ahead and a goal; 4 layers × 256; half of each batch mirrored; 200k iterations.
  • Generated courses: every part runs along the grid so walls stay straight; turns are 90° on a square landing; turning stairs are a flight, a landing turn and a flight.
  • The policy's ground input: 7 × 7 heights 5 cm apart around each foot's next planned landing, shifted up to 5 cm each episode; the 17 × 3 strip goes to the value network. Its pelvis height is above the ground under it (until 16 Nov it was the world height, metres off on graded and PARC clips).
  • The critic also sees the full reference pose in A; the policy never does.
  • B (planned): the command is a direction and speed along the course; patches at the guesser's next two footholds, shifted up to 5 cm each episode; episodes start from real poses; the guesser is refit from scratch every N updates on the latest rollouts.

References
  • SMP (Mu et al.): a frozen diffusion prior used as a reward.
  • PARC (Xu et al., SIGGRAPH 2025): generator and tracker trained together, with physics-corrected data added each round.
  • MaskedMimic (Tessler et al., SIGGRAPH Asia 2024): one controller from partial targets, learned from full motion capture.
  • Watch Your Step (Heyrman et al. 2026): terrain sampled only around predicted footholds learns faster than a whole map; the guesser is refit to the policy's own steps. Version B follows it.
  • Wang & Srinivasan 2014, "Stepping in the direction of the fall": the next lateral foothold follows from upper-body state during swing (the yardstick's basis).
  • Matthis, Yates & Hayhoe 2018, Current Biology: people look about two to three steps ahead at their footholds.
  • Bones SEED: Motion Data by Bones Studio (bones.studio), gated licence.

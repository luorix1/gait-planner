gait_planner · design proposal · 2026-11-20

The planner says where. The muscles decide how.

A diffusion planner learns whole-body human motion from mocap, but the muscle policy only receives a thin slice of it: where the pelvis goes, where and when each foot lands, and how the trunk leans. The policy picks its own joint motion to get there. Two kinds of check keep the planner's output within what our 90-muscle model can actually do.

The whole system

Generate the full body, send only the endpoints

The planner works like other motion-diffusion models and outputs every joint. We keep the blue parts and drop the grey joints before anything reaches the muscle policy. The two dashed paths at the bottom are the two ways the planner gets physically grounded.

Why train on full pose

Three things we get for free

More signal

Every joint supervises training

The sparse planner learned from about 15 numbers per frame and overfit after 7.5k iterations: held-out loss 0.029, then up to 0.05. A whole body per frame is far more to learn from on the same clips.

Free ablation

The interface is a slice

Pelvis, feet and trunk are all functions of the generated pose, so we can try different interfaces without retraining the planner. "How much must the planner tell the muscles?" becomes a clean paper result.

Same format back

Rollouts are full poses too

The simulator produces full, physically valid poses, so executed motion goes straight back into the planner's data, PARC-style. The muscle policy still only sees the slice.

    Physical grounding

    Why "just make the policy track it" is a trap

    The obvious loop is to have the muscle policy imitate each generated trajectory and punish the planner when it fails. Learning to imitate hard motions takes a long time, and a failure doesn't say what went wrong.

    The policy can't follow this plan

    After N training steps it still falls or misses the footholds.

    …because it needs more training

    A competence gap. Fix the policy, keep the plan.

    …because the body can't do it

    Not enough muscle, a force outside the friction cone, a center of mass that can't be supported. Fix the planner.

    The fix: answer the second branch with physics alone (Tier 1), and use the policy only in a way that never has to ask why it failed (Tier 2).

    Tier 1 · no policy, no training

    Check the generated full pose against physics and the muscles

    These checks need the full pose, which is one more reason to generate it. Each check either passes or fails, and there's no "maybe it needed more training". Most of it is differentiable, so it can also steer denoising.

    1 · Contacts

    Stance feet don't slide or sink. Swing feet carry no load.

    2 · Whole-body forces

    The ground force implied by the COM's acceleration, m·(a − g), must push up, stay inside the friction cone, and act under a stance foot.

    3 · Residual forces

    Inverse dynamics (SPRINT's RNEA) on pose plus contacts. A force needed at the pelvis that no foot can supply means the motion is dynamically inconsistent. This is OpenSim's RRA measure.

    4 · Muscle capacity

    Static optimization on those joint torques with our muscles' moment arms and strengths. Can every activation stay at or below 1?

    Use it three ways

    An evaluation metric for any planner. A filter that rejects bad samples. Guidance during denoising, using its gradient.

    Calibrate on real mocap first

    AB01 motions should pass. That sets the thresholds, and it also shows whether the model itself is too weak anywhere.

    What it can't see

    Static optimization ignores activation and tendon dynamics, and it can't tell whether the motion can be balanced with neural delays. So passing is necessary, not sufficient. Tier 2 covers the rest.

    Tier 2 · one generalist policy, trained once

    Learn from what the body actually did

    No per-trajectory imitation learning. One muscle policy that follows many plans runs the planner's samples, and we keep the plan it achieved instead of judging the plan it was given.

    Nobody has to explain a failure

    If the policy misses a foothold by 8 cm, the recorded foothold is where the foot really landed. The planner learns that version. It doesn't matter whether the miss came from a weak policy or an impossible plan.

    The data problem shrinks

    Each round adds physically valid motion, which also works against the overfitting we saw.

    A filter at run time

    The policy's critic, V(state, plan), estimates whether this body can execute this plan from here. Sample K plans at each replan and keep the best. Cheap, and no retraining.

    Precedent

    PARC runs this loop with kinematic motion; ours stays at the plan level. PhysDiff (ICCV 2023) puts a generalist tracker in the simulator inside the denoising loop, which is heavier than this.

    Open decisions

    Planner goal. Today it's the clip's COM travel over 1.2 s. For the study it's probably a command (speed and heading, maybe a target point) plus the terrain ahead.

    How human-like the policy must be. Closely matching AB01 kinematics and EMG keeps a tracking reward. "Human-like style" means AMP plus EMG, with following the plan as the task.

    Batched moment arms. Before committing to static optimization on the GPU, check that SPRINT exposes per-pose moment arms in batch.

    Why not generate poses and track them directly (the PARC route)

    A generated pose sequence can contain transitions our muscle model can't make. Tracking it frame by frame forces the policy to try anyway. Sending only endpoints lets the policy choose a feasible joint motion that meets the same goals. The full pose is still useful inside the planner, and for the physics checks.
